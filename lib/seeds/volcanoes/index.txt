A volcano is not really a mountain but a hole: a place where molten rock, gas and ash escape from the planet to the surface, with the mountain merely the debris pile built around it. Earth has roughly 1,300 volcanoes that have erupted within the last 10,000 years, and around 50 to 70 erupt in any given year. Most are hidden. About three quarters of the planet's volcanic output happens unseen along the mid-ocean ridges, where new seafloor is manufactured continuously, while the famous cones of the Pacific "Ring of Fire" mark the places where old seafloor dives back into the mantle.

## Why the rock melts

The mantle beneath our feet is almost entirely solid, so a volcano needs a trick to melt it. [[Plate tectonics]] supplies three. At ridges, plates pull apart and the mantle rising to fill the gap loses pressure, which lets it melt without any added heat. At subduction zones, the sinking slab carries water down with it, and that water lowers the melting point of the rock above, producing the sticky, gas-rich magma that feeds explosive arcs like the Andes and Japan. And at a [[Hotspot (geology)|hotspot]], a plume of unusually hot rock rises from deep within, punching through the plate above; the Hawaiian chain is the trail left as the Pacific plate slid over one such plume.

The chemistry of the magma decides the character of the eruption. Runny basalt lets gas bubble out gently, so Hawaiian volcanoes pour lava in rivers and build vast, shallow shields. Silica-rich magma is thick as cold tar and traps its gas until the pressure shatters it, which is why the same planet produces both tourist-friendly lava flows and detonations that reshape continents' weather.

## When the mountain explodes

The deadliest volcanic weapon is rarely lava, which moves slowly enough to walk away from. It is the [[Pyroclastic flow]], an avalanche of incandescent ash and gas that can hurtle downhill at hundreds of kilometres per hour. One of these buried [[Pompeii]] and Herculaneum in AD 79 after Vesuvius first rained pumice for hours, and later surges killed those who had stayed behind. Scientists rank eruptions on the [[Volcanic Explosivity Index]], a logarithmic scale where each step is roughly ten times more material, topping out at 8 for supereruptions.

The 1883 eruption of [[Krakatoa]] shows what the upper end of that scale does to the atmosphere. Its bangs were heard about 4,800 km away, on Rodrigues Island in the Indian Ocean, and barometers around the world recorded the pressure wave circling the globe several times, with the blast registering seven times in all. The associated tsunamis killed an estimated 36,000 people, and the sunsets stayed lurid for months afterward.

^ Violent eruptions can also generate [[Volcanic lightning]], as colliding charged ash particles electrify the plume. Bolts flicker through the column at Krakatoa-scale events and in Iceland's ash clouds.

## Climate, myth and treasure

Ash falls quickly, but sulfur dioxide lofted into the stratosphere turns to a haze of sulfuric acid droplets that reflects sunlight for a year or two. Tambora's 1815 eruption cooled the planet enough to cause the [[Year Without a Summer]] of 1816, with crop failures across Europe and North America. [[Mount Pinatubo]] in 1991 lowered global temperatures by roughly half a degree Celsius, a natural experiment that climate modellers still use. Some researchers argue that the [[Toba catastrophe theory]], a supereruption about 74,000 years ago, nearly wiped out early humans, though genetic and archaeological evidence for that remains disputed.

The same violence has left legacies. The [[Minoan eruption]] of Thera around 1600 BC buried a Bronze Age town at Akrotiri and has long been proposed as a seed for the Atlantis story, though that link is speculation. Deep, fast-rising [[Kimberlite]] eruptions, which explode up from more than 150 km down, are how nearly all gem diamonds reach the surface. Elsewhere in the solar system, [[Olympus Mons]] on Mars is a shield volcano about 22 km high, and Jupiter's moon [[Io (moon)|Io]] is the most volcanically active body known, its eruptions powered by tidal flexing rather than plate motion.
