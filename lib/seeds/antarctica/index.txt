Antarctica is the coldest, driest and windiest continent on Earth, and about 98 per cent of it is buried under ice. That ice averages nearly two kilometres thick, lifting the land high above sea level, and the interior gets so little snow that it counts as a desert. In winter, temperatures there can fall below minus 60 degrees Celsius.

^ Greenland's ice would raise the sea about 7 metres; Antarctica's holds roughly eight times that.

## Ice that could move the sea

If all of its ice melted, the [[Antarctic ice sheet]] would raise global sea level by about 58 metres, a figure known as its sea-level equivalent. Most of that volume sits on East Antarctica, but much of West Antarctica rests on bedrock below sea level, where warm ocean water can reach its underside. Where that water undercuts the ice, the point where it lifts off the bed can retreat inland, and some models show that retreat speeding up once it starts. [[Thwaites Glacier]], in West Antarctica, is the focus of that worry and has been nicknamed the Doomsday Glacier in the press.

## Records in the ice

Snow that compacts into ice traps tiny bubbles of ancient air, so a drilled core reads the atmosphere like a diary. The European EPICA project drilled more than three kilometres down at Dome C, and its core reaches back about 800,000 years, through eight glacial cycles of the [[ice ages|Ice ages]]. Its bubbles show carbon dioxide swinging between about 180 and 280 parts per million, rising and falling with each cycle's temperature. At [[Vostok Station]], a thermometer recorded minus 89.2 degrees Celsius on 21 July 1983, the lowest reliable reading on record.

## Darkness, cold and unusual animals

For about six months each year the sun never rises at the pole, and in that [[polar night|Polar night]] the [[southern lights|Northern lights]] glow in the sky. The aurora australis forms when charged particles from the Sun meet the upper atmosphere, the same process that lights the Arctic at the other end of the planet. The brightest displays tend to follow solar storms, when the Sun hurls extra particles toward Earth.

[[Emperor penguin|Emperor penguins]] breed through this darkness. Each female lays a single egg and heads out to sea to feed, while the male keeps it balanced on his feet under a fold of belly skin, fasting for about two months. The chicks hatch in late winter, when the light is only just returning.

In the Southern Ocean, [[icefish|Icefish]] have no haemoglobin and no red blood cells, so their blood looks almost colourless. They carry oxygen dissolved in the plasma instead, and their survival in water close to freezing depends on [[antifreeze proteins|Antifreeze protein]] that bind to ice crystals and stop them growing. Cold water holds more dissolved oxygen than warm water, which makes the trick workable.

## Meteorites and a shared continent

Meteorites that land on the ice sheet are carried along by its slow flow, and wind-scoured [[blue ice|Blue ice (glaciology)]] brings them back to the surface. Those patches have yielded tens of thousands of meteorites since searches began in the 1960s. One of them, found in 1981, is a fragment of the Moon that reached Earth by impact, unlike the rocks that [[Apollo 11]] astronauts carried home from the Moon.

Seven countries have territorial claims on Antarctica, and the [[Antarctic Treaty]] froze those claims rather than settling them. The treaty, signed in 1959 by twelve nations, reserves the continent for peaceful purposes and science. A 1991 protocol added a ban on mining that runs until 2048, so the continent's minerals are off limits for now.
